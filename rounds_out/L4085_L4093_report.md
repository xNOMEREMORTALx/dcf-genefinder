### A proved theorem found here [P] (noticed after looking, so not a passed prediction)
Several of the centred hexagonal members were 37, 3367, 333667, 33…3 66…67. These are no accident:

**Theorem.** For every k ≥ 1, with n = (10ᵏ + 2)/3, the centred hexagonal number Hₙ = 3n(n−1)+1 equals (10²ᵏ + 10ᵏ + 1)/3, and its reciprocal has **exact period 3k**.

**Proof.**
1. **The identity is direct algebra.**
2. **The period divides 3k.** Since 10³ᵏ − 1 = (10ᵏ − 1)(10²ᵏ + 10ᵏ + 1) = 3·Hₙ·(10ᵏ − 1), Hₙ divides 10³ᵏ − 1.
3. **The period is at least 2k.** If the period d were smaller, Hₙ would divide 10ᵈ − 1 < 10ᵈ. But Hₙ > 10²ᵏ/3 > 10²ᵏ⁻¹, so d ≥ 2k.
4. **So the period is 3k.** The only divisor of 3k that is at least 2k is 3k itself. ∎

**Checked by computation for k = 1–12.**
- **k = 1 gives 37 = H₄** (also the star number S₃), period 3.
- **k = 2 gives 3367**, period 6.
- **k = 10 gives 33333333336666666667**, period 30.

**Also seen [D]:**
- 2791 = H₃₁ has period 31.
- 35113 = 13·37·73 (the product of three consecutive star numbers) has period 24.

**What this means.** Centred hexagonal numbers do sit at reciprocal periods 3k through a real algebraic family, 37's family. The *density* test still passes because the family is sparse: one member per multiple of 3.
