## 1. Neurons and the visual microphone (L4029 void, L4045 2 of 5)
**Anchor.** Ling et al. (Light Sci Appl 7:107, 2018; H8 cited this as PNAS) report cell deformations up to 3 nm during the action potential, seen by interferometric phase imaging at up to 50,000 frames/s.

**Model.**
- Hodgkin–Huxley neurons (20 °C) feed the membrane deformation.
- A "phone on a microscope" camera images it: 240 fps, 100 nm per pixel, 2 DN noise.
- The real `MotionMeter` estimator calibrates the noise (gate G1 passed: gain 0.99, noise within 9%).
- Detection is a matched filter at known spike times; spike-triggered averaging adds spikes.

| scene / mode | single-spike threshold | 10,000-spike threshold |
|---|---|---|
| textured scene, 1/240 s exposure (L4029, seen) | 15.6 nm | 0.15 nm |
| **real-looking cell edge (10 DN contrast)**, 1/240 s | **138 nm** | **1.27 nm** |
| cell edge, 0.25 ms exposure, global frames | 120 nm | 0.37 nm |
| cell edge, 0.25 ms exposure, **rolling shutter** | **17 nm** | 0.17 nm |

**What passed and failed (L4045):**
- **V1 FAIL.**
  - I predicted a > 10× loss on a real cell edge and invisibility even after 10⁴ spikes.
  - The loss was 8.9×, and 10⁴ averaged spikes reach 1.3 nm, below Ling's 3 nm.
  - **In this idealised model**, motion-estimation precision is *not* the barrier.
- **V2 PASS.** Rolling shutter with short exposure beats global frames by **7×** (5× in L4029, replicated on a new scene).
  - **Reason:** rolling readout samples time continuously, row by row, while a short global exposure catches a 0.4 ms spike only about 6% of the time.
  - This is a usable design rule for any brief event.
- **V3 FAIL.** K⁺ channel speed was *not* readable from shape.
  - Recovered width ratio slowK/HH: 1.10, against a true 1.56.
  - A 0.25 ms exposure plus 0.25 ms smoothing blurs 0.4–0.7 ms spikes together.
  - Shape needs exposures well below spike width (tens of µs). That is high-speed-camera territory, not a phone.
- **V4 FAIL.** My width estimator's ±2 ms window caps widths below 4 ms, so the ≥ 3 ms prediction could not hold (design flaw).
- **V5 PASS.** The new **tremor mode** recovers 9.30 Hz at 30, 60 and 240 fps.

**Honest limits of the model (why this does not mean a phone can see neurons):**
1. The real deformation is mostly *axial* (towards the camera); bright-field intensity barely changes with nm axial motion. Ling needed phase imaging.
2. Nanometre stage drift and hand or building vibration are not modelled.
3. Averaging needs each spike's time from an electrode or optogenetic trigger.

**Conclusion.** DCF's neuron path is two-track:
- **Now:** neuron-*driven* motion a phone can see. Tremor mode ships as **phone menu 38 (bundle 6.21, self-test 270/270)**. Not for medical use.
- **Partner:** interferometric phase imaging labs record impulses; DCF contributes the K⁺-channel inventory (`dcf_nerve`), the rolling/short-exposure sampling rule, and spike-shape analysis.

## 2. The genome reader on long-read-style assemblies (L4041, 6 of 7)
**Setup.**
- **Data:** NC_000932, the *Arabidopsis* chloroplast (154,478 bp, unseen), from the Biopython 1.84 source distribution, because NCBI is unreachable here.
- **Errors:** nanopore-like residual errors (80% homopolymer ±1 indels), three replicates per rate.

| error rate | found | correct | exact start | predicted genes | code call |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 78.6% | 31.3% | 59.3% | 179 | 11 |
| 1e-4 (polished) | 79.0% | 32.3% | 55.5% | 185 | 11 ×3 |
| 1e-3 | 70.5% | 37.9% | 43.8% | 223 | 11 ×3 |
| 5e-3 | 45.7% | 46.0% | 18.4% | 309 | 11 ×3 |

**Passed:**
- LR0 baseline;
- LR1 no loss on polished assemblies;
- LR2 −8 points at 1e-3, as predicted from "one fragment still covers half the gene";
- LR3 −33 points at 5e-3;
- LR4 1.7× more (fragmented) genes;
- LR5 the code call held in all 10 runs.

**LR6 FAIL (my design error).** The built-in frameshift detector only runs on genomes ≥ 500 kb (`L>=500000`), so it never ran on this 154 kb genome.

**Also seen:**
- **Precision on an organelle genome is low** (correct 31%). The gene finder's own RELIABILITY WARNING (median gene 189 nt) fired correctly.
- **"Correct" rises with the error rate.** This is an artefact: fragments inside real genes count as correct.
- **Exact starts are the most fragile measure** (59% → 18%).

## 3. `dcf_anchor.py`: the Manuscript 3 test as a tool (L4037 1/3, L4049 2/4, L4053 2/3)
**The tool.** `dcf_anchor.py` (numpy only, MIT) packages the L3725/L3825a/L3869/L3909 machinery:
- the GENCODE gene table;
- TSS within 5 kb of an anchor;
- 50 nearest genes in length and density;
- 10,000 resampled sets, all maps scored together;
- ENCODE "top", 4DN and all-loop formats, and CTCF-reference restriction;
- new diagnostics: covariate **balance** and **effective distinct controls**.

**Self-tests on synthetic genomes** (anchoring rises with gene length; test genes drawn from the long tail):
- **The confounder is real.** A naive test is fooled 99 times in 100 (A3 pass); matching removes almost all of it.
- **But the matched test over-rejects.** False positives at nominal 5%: 13, 13 and 6 per 100 on three independent seeds, pooled **10.7%** (P = 5×10⁻⁵ against 5%).
- **Power to detect a +0.15 planted excess:** 14–16 of 20 (I predicted ≥ 18).
- **Mechanisms tested and rejected as the main cause:**
  - the residual length gap (k = 10 cut it from 0.074 to 0.022 SD, but false positives stayed 13/100);
  - the pool-mean sampling error (about 770 effective distinct controls, so the variance inflation is only about 8%).
- **The variance-inflated p (`p_upper_adj`)** gave 8/100 on the L4053 seed (C2 pass). The cause of the excess is still open.

**Consequence for Manuscript 3 (important).**
- Its matched-gene p-values may be too small by up to about 2× when the gene family sits in a tail of the length or density distribution.
- The strongest results (p = 0.0008–0.008) would survive a 2× correction, but that must be *shown*, not assumed.
- **The decisive check needs the real files:** run the same test on ≥ 200 placebo families (random non-channel gene sets matched to KPORE's covariate profile) in each real map, and report the empirical false-positive rate and calibrated p-values beside the frozen ones.
- In real data longer genes sit at anchors *less* (L3853), the opposite of the synthetic design, so the real bias may even run against the K⁺ excess. The placebo test settles it.

## Summary
- **A phone cannot see nerve impulses as built.** The barrier is optics (axial motion, drift, spike timing), not motion-estimation precision: 10⁴ averaged spikes reach about 1 nm in an idealised model.
- **Rolling shutter with short exposure beats global frames 5–7×.** That design rule carries over to any brief event.
- **The neuron-driven signal a phone *can* read, tremor, now ships as menu 38** (bundle 6.21, 270/270).
- **The genome reader works on polished long-read assemblies, loses 8 points at 0.1% error and 33 at 0.5%, and keeps its code call.** Its frameshift detector is limited to genomes ≥ 500 kb.
- **`dcf_anchor.py` exists and is honest about itself.** Its self-test shows the matched test over-rejects about 2× on tail gene sets, which Manuscript 3 must now calibrate on real data.
